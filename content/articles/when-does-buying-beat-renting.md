Buying a house does not beat renting on day one. It beats renting somewhere around
year four or five, and that crossover point moves a lot depending on your rent, the
price of the house, and the interest rate.

If you might move before you reach it, renting probably leaves you better off.

## Why renting wins early

Two things work against you in the first few years of owning.

The first is the money you hand over on day one. On a $400,000 house you need about
$92,000 up front once you add closing costs to a 20% deposit. A renter still has
that money. If they leave it invested, it is earning while the buyer's is tied up in
a house.

The second is that early mortgage payments are mostly interest. You are paying the
bank for the loan, not buying much of the house. Add property tax, insurance and
repairs, and the first few years of owning build less than people expect.

Renting also has a real cost that is easy to forget. Rent goes up, roughly 3% a year,
and it never stops. A mortgage payment does not move.

## Where the line actually falls

Here is a specific case. Rent of $2,200 a month against a $400,000 house at 6.5%,
with 20% down.

Owning costs about $2,523 a month once you include tax, insurance and upkeep, so it
runs about $323 a month more than renting. This is what each path is worth if you
cash out at the end of the year:

| Year | Renting | Owning | Ahead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $98,817 | $71,013 | Renting |
| 3 | $111,192 | $102,828 | Renting |
| 5 | $121,674 | $137,213 | Owning |
| 7 | $130,000 | $174,424 | Owning |
| 10 | $142,226 | $240,594 | Owning |
| 15 | $165,212 | $387,411 | Owning |

![Renting is ahead for the first four years, then owning overtakes it and the gap widens](/articles/rent-vs-buy-crossover.svg)

Buying overtakes renting in **year 4**. After that the gap widens fast, because the
renter's costs keep climbing while the owner's payment stays flat.

Both columns count the same money. The renter has the deposit invested plus whatever
they save each month. The owner has equity after paying the costs of selling.

## What moves the line

**The interest rate.** This is the big one.

| Mortgage rate | Buying pulls ahead in |
| --- | --- |
| 4% | year 3 |
| 5% | year 3 |
| 6.5% | year 4 |
| 7% | year 5 |
| 8% | year 7 |

Going from 4% to 8% on the same house pushes the crossover out by four years.

**How expensive renting is where you live.** Same house, different rents:

| Monthly rent | Buying pulls ahead in |
| --- | --- |
| $1,600 | year 10 |
| $1,900 | year 6 |
| $2,200 | year 4 |
| $2,500 | year 3 |

If you have a cheap rental, staying put is worth more than people admit. If your rent
is high, that is what makes buying urgent, not the house itself.

## The part that quietly decides it

All of this assumes the renter actually invests the difference.

If the deposit sits in a checking account, and the few hundred a month that owning
would have cost gets absorbed into everyday spending, then renting loses on every
line of that table. Not because renting is worse, but because nothing was saved.

That is the real fork in the road. Renting only wins if the money you did not spend
on a house goes somewhere and stays there.
