## What This Article Covers

Welcome to House Planner. This article is a quick guide to thinking about one of life's biggest decisions: whether to rent or buy, and if you buy, how soon you can afford to.

## Why You Might Rent

Renting gives you flexibility. If your job moves, your lease ends and you can go. You're not responsible for major repairs, the landlord is. Your housing cost is predictable month to month (though rent does tend to rise over time).

Renting also lets you stay liquid. Money that would go to a down payment, property taxes, or maintenance stays in your pocket or gets invested elsewhere.

## Why You Might Buy

Buying builds equity. Instead of paying a landlord, your mortgage payment builds ownership. Over decades, that can mean owning an asset worth hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Buying is a hedge against inflation. Your mortgage payment stays fixed; your rent usually goes up. After a few years, your fixed housing cost looks better and better as the cost of living rises.

Buying also gives you control. You can renovate, paint, plant a garden. It's yours.

## The Real Question

It's not "rent or buy", it's "rent or buy *now*." The calculator helps you model both paths:

1. **Keep renting** for the next N years while you save.
2. **Buy as soon as you hit** the targets (down payment saved, payment affordable).

For many people, the answer is: rent for a few more years while you build the down payment, *then* buy. The calculator shows you that timeline.

## How to Use This Calculator

Start at the top:

1. **Enter your income.** Take-home pay, or gross salary + state for an estimate.
2. **Add your expenses.** Rent, utilities, groceries, everything.
3. **Set your house savings.** How much each month goes toward the down payment.
4. **Enter your debt** (if any). The calculator figures out how long until you're debt-free.
5. **Enter a home price and mortgage rate.** The calculator compares 15-year and 30-year loans and tells you when you can afford to buy.

At the bottom, you'll see your optimal plan: the soonest date you can responsibly buy, with enough down payment and a payment under the affordability line.

## The Numbers

The calculator uses real tax brackets and rates for federal and state income tax. Mortgage calculations assume a fixed rate. Closing costs, PMI (private mortgage insurance), and property tax are all baked in.

**Important:** These are estimates for planning. Not tax advice, not financial advice. Always talk to a tax pro, lender, or financial advisor before making a move.
